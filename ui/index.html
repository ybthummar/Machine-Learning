      How it works

      Original CSVRead the restaurant records with pandas.
      Training rowsscikit-learn fits two decision trees on roughly 80% of eligible rows.
      Test rowsThe other 20% measure how well each tree works on held-out records.
      Live predictionYour inputs follow one path through the trained tree to a result.
      Rating prediction · decision tree regression

      Regression estimates a number. The tree learns questions about cost for two, price tier, votes, table booking and online delivery. Each answer sends the row down a branch. The final group of training rows supplies the average rating used as the prediction.

      We train with rated listings and leave out rating text and color because the dataset derives them from the answer. A vote count is available for listings with existing reviews. Costs use the units recorded in the CSV.

      Cuisine prediction · decision tree classification

      Classification picks a label. The tree uses the restaurant name, encoded as an ID-like number, and the cost for two. It returns the most common full cuisine label in the final group of training rows. “Japanese, Sushi” is one label and must match as a whole.

      Restaurant names and prices give the model limited clues. Two listings with the same name can appear in both training and test rows.

      Recommendations and city charts · rules and summaries

      Recommendations use the notebook’s cuisine-matching rule. A full-label match scores 1 and any other label scores 0. The app keeps the highest-rated listing per restaurant name, excludes the chosen restaurant, and shows up to five matches.

      The city charts group rows by city, count listings, and average the ratings of listings that have one. The coordinate plot shows where listings appear in the CSV. The counts describe the dataset’s coverage.

      What we use to build the app

      Python runs the app locally. pandas reads and groups the CSV. scikit-learn trains the trees and measures test performance. HTML, CSS, and JavaScript build the forms and charts and request predictions from Python when you click Run.

      What to say in a presentation

      “I start with Ooma’s real record. The rating model predicts a number, and the cuisine model predicts a label. I compare both with what was recorded. Then I use a matching rule to find similar restaurants and group the original records to show what the dataset covers in Ooma’s city.”
