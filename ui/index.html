How it works

Rating prediction

A decision tree learns from the cost for two, price tier, vote count, and booking and delivery options. It estimates ratings for restaurants with those characteristics.

Training excludes zero ratings, which the dataset labels as “Not rated,” and rating text and color, which are derived from the rating. MAE measures the average error in rating points. R² measures how much variation the model explains on the held-out rows. Votes are available for listings with existing reviews.

Cuisine classification

A decision tree uses the encoded restaurant name and cost for two to predict a full cuisine label, such as “Japanese, Sushi.” Choose a known restaurant in Original data to try it.

Accuracy is the share of held-out rows with an exact label match. Names and prices alone provide limited information about cuisine, and names can occur in both training and test rows.

Recommendations

The notebook represents each complete cuisine label as one category. Its Jaccard similarity is 1 for identical labels and 0 otherwise. The UI uses that matching rule, keeps the highest-rated row for each candidate name, and shows up to five results ranked by rating.

Location analysis

City views count original records, plot the supplied coordinates, and summarize cuisine and price tiers. Average ratings use rated rows. Cuisine counts split comma-separated labels, so one restaurant can count toward several cuisines.

City totals describe dataset coverage. Costs are displayed with the currency listed in the source and are used as recorded by the models.
